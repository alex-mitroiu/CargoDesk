01

Bottom line

Four weeks on, the FCL workflow is no longer the story — the commercial layer is. CargoDesk now buys space the way forwarders actually do: contract space steered in order, weekly allocations, carrier spot offers and margin rules. It also runs a deeper finance stack and speaks DCSA on every carrier message. The deciding gap has not moved: no carrier is on the other end of the wire.

1

Still zero live carriers — and now it's the one thing missing

The Booking, Track & Trace and Schedules adapters were rebuilt against DCSA's published specs, and every message is checked against them. A built-in DCSA carrier stands in for any carrier without an integration. The database on disk holds zero carrier integrations, so every booking and spot offer today comes from that demo carrier.

One carrier sandbox (Hapag-Lloyd's is public self-service) would prove three built capabilities at once: booking, events and schedules.

2

The commercial layer now matches what the big suites sell as modules

Allocation steering covers what Descartes markets as Allocation Management and CargoWise as Ocean Carrier Contracts and Allocations: weekly space, Where to book, out-of-order reasons and a steering rate. Spot offers with margin rules cover what their rate-management modules do, except the live carrier spot feed.

3

Visibility is still vessel-level in practice

The pipe for container events now exists: DCSA Track & Trace ingestion feeds container events and milestones. But nothing flows through it, because no live carrier is connected and the demo carrier doesn't send events. Gate in/out and discharge are still typed by hand, which is still the gap project44 and FourKites charge a premium to close.

04

Where CargoDesk leads or matches

Not just a gap list — eight places the evidence supports parity with, or an edge over, named competitors.

Freight Audit matching depth

Independent DET/DEM free-time windows and a safeguard against silently overwriting an already-posted cost line; since October it also matches against spot-offer lines. Conceptually matches CargoWise's own described AI three-way match, and appears to exceed what Magaya documents publicly.

AES/EEI export-control depth

ECCN, Schedule B, license type/value per cargo line, plus a live staleness check if the shipment changes after filing — comparable to Descartes' dedicated compliance product line, built as one feature inside a general TMS.

VGM's full SOLAS cycle

Method 1/Method 2 declaration with a real submit/accept/reject cycle and milestone auto-completion, matching what CargoWise and Magaya describe as standard.

Quote-to-shipment conversion

One atomic transaction carries over parties, derives containers, creates SELL and BUY cost lines, checks credit hold, screens for denied parties, and recomputes the space badge. Since October, a quote bought on a carrier spot offer carries that offer through as the BUY side and the booking's quotation reference. This is deeper than what Magaya's public materials describe for the same handoff.

The AIS integration is real, not vaporware

A live third-party feed doing genuine geofence-based departure/arrival detection — the same underlying technique project44 uses, at a fraction of the coverage. Worth saying plainly so it isn't lumped in with "everything is simulated."

Multi-entity accounting

FX-aware margin by entity with branch scoping, plus GL export to any accounting system, FX revaluation and VAT per charge line, is enterprise-grade. It is likely ahead of Magaya's more SMB-oriented accounting.

Allocation steering inside the booking flow

Space is held per week or per period on each contract line. Where to book walks the steering order with free space, a booking against it records a reason, and the Steering tab gives a rate per lane and carrier. This is the same “steer operations to book on the agreement” Descartes sells as Allocation Management.

Standards discipline on every carrier message

Every DCSA message sent or received is validated against DCSA's own published specs, kept in the repo. The built-in DCSA carrier answers the way a real one would, so connecting a carrier will mostly be configuration, not rework.

05

The real gaps, classified

Split the way the last competitive pass split its findings: work that's genuinely engineering, and work that no amount of engineering closes alone. One buildable gap closed since September: DCSA Track & Trace ingestion was built on 9 October.

Buildable

Real engineering effort, bounded and scoped
  • Connect and validate one real carrier sandboxBooking, Track & Trace and Schedules are all coded to DCSA's specs and waiting. Hapag-Lloyd's sandbox is public self-service, so this is mostly a signup step, with a modest tail to fix real-spec deviations.
  • Live carrier spot APIsThe spot-rates adapter shape exists and the demo carrier fills it. Hapag-Lloyd Quick Quotes and the Maersk Offers API need access; Maersk's is approval-gated.
  • DCSA everywhere, phases 2–9Covers Shipping Instructions and transport documents (eBL 3.0), VGM 1.0, Track & Trace 3.0, operational vessel schedules, Arrival Notice and Port Call. Planned phase by phase, with the demo carrier playing each one so the flows can be tested before a carrier connects.
  • Freight Audit matching-engine breadthMore charge codes and D&D edge cases — incremental extension of an engine that already works.
  • Direct AES/EEI government EDI transmissionA defined, documented API (AESDirect/ACE). Needs filer credentials from the business, then a bounded build following the DCSA adapter's own pattern.

Structural — not a code gap

Years of partnerships or a data business, not a sprint
  • CargoWise/Descartes-scale carrier network breadth20+ years of bilateral EDI certifications with hundreds of carriers. A network-effects moat, not a backlog item.
  • project44/FourKites-style global visibility98%+ ocean freight, 700+ ports — needs commercial data deals with terminals and carriers worldwide.
  • CargoSphere/Freightos-style rate benchmarkingThe product exists only because it pools real transaction data across thousands of forwarders. A single-tenant platform has no comparable pool.
  • Legally negotiable eB/LNeeds accreditation with a title registry banks and customs trust as authoritative — a legal/network problem, not a coding one.

Detailed evidence

Full citations behind every matrix row — collapsed by default, open any that need checking. Entries marked Updated or New were checked on disk on 21 October. The others keep September's file and line references (v0.91.7), which may have shifted since.

Quote → booking conversion Full

routes/quotes.js:1-52 — real Draft→Sent→Accepted/Declined/Expired→Converted lifecycle with an hourly auto-expire sweep. Conversion (:329-484) is atomic: carries shipper/consignee/principal/notify parties, derives containers from quote lines (:391-426), creates SELL cost lines from the agreed price (:380-389) and BUY lines from the matched contract (:378), auto-assigns carrier line agents (:377), recomputes the space/TEU badge (:433), runs denied-party screening (:435), and surfaces a credit-hold warning across all three carried-over parties (:441-446).

Carrier booking workflow Full workflow0 live

Updated 21 Oct.

  • The adapter (lib/carrier-integrations/dcsa-bkg-v2.js) was rebuilt on 9 Oct against the spec files in DCSA's Conformance Gateway. The September version missed six of the booking request's eight required fields, and sent an internal container code where ISO 6346 is required. It now also covers amend, cancel and polling.
  • Validation: every message sent or received is checked against DCSA's specs, kept in lib/dcsa/specs/ (lib/dcsa/validate.js).
  • The demo carrier: a carrier with no integration of its own is played by the built-in DCSA carrier (lib/dcsa-simulator/). It is reached over HTTP like a real one and badged DEMO CARRIER.
  • Confirmation: manual confirmation and eAdapter were retired on 11 Oct. Only the carrier's DCSA confirmation confirms a booking (applyCarrierState, routes/edi.js).
  • Escalations: a rejected, changed or unanswered booking can be escalated, with the Lane Steering order offered as alternatives. A no-answer switch moves it on by itself (lib/booking-escalations.js).
  • Requested vs Carrier: every confirmation is compared detail by detail with the request (lib/carrier-confirmation.js).
  • Tests: dcsa-lifecycle, booking-escalations and main-vessel-carrier-changes, among 107 backend files passing on 21 Oct.
Schedules & routing Full

Updated 21 Oct. The internal schedule catalog and mock fallback are unchanged (routes/system.js). Every sailing now names its main vessel, the leg with the longest transit, and marks its feeders. The shipment's vessel follows the main vessel (lib/mainLeg.js). The DCSA Commercial Schedules search was rebuilt to the spec (searchSchedules in dcsa-bkg-v2.js) but has not run against a real carrier.

VGM — SOLAS Method 1/2 Full

Pending→Submitted is blocked without a declared Method 1 (Weighing) or Method 2 (Calculated) (routes/shipments.js:1009-1021; UI at src/pages/shipments/VgmServicePage.jsx:45-46,82-83) →Accepted/Rejected via a dedicated simulator (:1132-1168), with a vgm_declaration EDI message logged on submit (:1111-1122, explicitly simulated) and automatic milestone completion once every container is Submitted (:1123-1126). Updated 21 Oct: still simulated; DCSA VGM 1.0 is a later phase of the DCSA-everywhere plan.

Shipping Instructions Full, simulated

routes/shipping-instructions.js implements an independent Draft→Submitted→Confirmed/Rejected machine (mirrors customs-filing's shape, :1-9), gated on shipper/consignee/container completeness (:62-65), auto-completing the si_submitted milestone (:81), with reset-and-resubmit after rejection (:116-127). Explicitly disclosed as simulated, no live carrier EDI (:8). Updated 21 Oct: unchanged; DCSA Shipping Instructions and transport documents (eBL 3.0) are planned.

AES/EEI & ISF/AMS filing Full

routes/customs-filing.js supports both AES/EEI (export) and ISF/AMS (import) as independent filing types per shipment (UNIQUE(shipment_id, filing_type), :1-12), each with its own Draft→Filed→Accepted/Rejected cycle, required-broker-role gate (:117-119), priced-cargo gate, and USPPI/Ultimate-Consignee validation (:126-130). Export-control fields (Schedule B, ECCN, license type/value) come from container_packages (:39-53) and are snapshotted at filing time. A live staleness check recomputes whether the snapshot still matches the shipment's current carrier/vessel/voyage/cargo (:56-70). ISF/AMS is confirmed present as filing type ISF_AMS — easy to miss on a bare name search. Simulated only, no live AESDirect/ACE transmission (:11-12).

House B/L lifecycle Partial

BL01 auto-completes the bl_issued milestone (routes/shipment-ops.js:1263; regression-tested in tests/house-bl-lifecycle.test.js:118-140, including that a manually-completed milestone is never overwritten). Mark Surrendered / Mark Released are idempotent and logged as entity events (tests/house-bl-lifecycle.test.js:142-162). A bl_release_type field distinguishes Original/Telex Release/Surrendered/Seaway Bill (routes/shipments.js:77). No electronic B/L transfer (Bolero/essDOCS/WaveBL-style) exists anywhere in the codebase.

Milestones & real-time tracking Partial

Updated 21 Oct.

  • Vessels: departure and arrival are still confirmed live from AIS (lib/ais-listener.js).
  • New since September: DCSA Track & Trace ingestion (routes/carrier-tracking.js, mapping in lib/carrier-integrations/tnt-mapping.js). It pulls a booking's equipment and transport events, on demand or in a periodic sweep. It records them through the same recordContainerEvent as a manual entry, so milestones complete the same way and a re-pull never duplicates.
  • Why it's still Partial: ingestion needs an integration with the tracking capability, and none exists. The built-in DCSA carrier offers only booking and spot rates (lib/dcsa-simulator/index.js). In practice, container events are still entered by hand.
Freight Audit & Payment Full

routes/carrier-invoices.js reconciles a carrier's invoice line against an already-accrued cost line where one exists (:128-143) or the live contract rate otherwise (:145-154). Detention and Demurrage are distinct charge types with independent free-time windows and independent container_events anchor pairs (:91-125) — a previously-fixed bug is noted in the code where both used to share one window (:96-98). A configurable variance-tolerance percentage (:170) drives matched/variance classification; an exception queue surfaces worst-variance-first, capped at 200 rows (:200-226). Approving a line actualizes the matched cost line or creates a new one, and refuses to silently overwrite an already-posted line (:401-417). Full dispute capture and audit-event logging throughout. Updated 21 Oct: a spot offer's BUY lines are an expected source too (“vs. spot offer”, routes/carrier-invoices.js).

Invoicing, credit notes & multi-entity accounting Full

FR01 invoice and CN01 credit/debit note generation are both real (src/utils/invoiceGenerator.js:413,422,429,443,449), backed by an invoice-reversal test suite and an invoice-collections/billing-performance UI. Multi-entity/branch accounting is real: branch-level currency, FX-aware margin-by-entity resolved from the shipment's EMO/IMO office's branch, and branch-scoped visibility for non-global users (tests/multi-entity-accounting.test.js:1-8, routes/organization.js, src/pages/org/BranchPage.jsx).

  • GL Export to a generic journal-entry file (routes/gl-export.js).

Denied-party screening Full

Two syncable list sources: OFAC SDN and the Consolidated Screening List (routes/sanctions.js, parseOfacCsv, syncOfacSdn/syncConsolidatedScreeningList). Sync/import is admin-only and rate-limited after a fixed privilege-escalation bug (a viewer-role account could previously wipe the dataset, :24-35). A dedicated multi-list regression test exists (tests/multi-list-screening.test.js). Comparable in list coverage to Magaya's dedicated DPS product; CSL is a superset of most of what Magaya lists separately.

Carrier spot rates Partial

New, 21 Oct (unreleased).

  • The record (spot_offers, lib/spot-offers.js, routes/spot-offers.js): the reference; a classic quote or a digital offer; charges per container or per B/L, with “subject to” lines; validity; price lock; and penalty terms.
  • On a quote, the offer fixes the carrier and becomes a SPOT shipment on conversion.
  • On a shipment, its charges become BUY lines, and the booking carries its reference as DCSA contractQuotationReference.
  • Guardrails: an expired digital offer can't be booked; a classic one only warns. Cancelling a confirmed spot booking shows the cancellation or no-show fee first and accrues it.
  • Margin rules by customer, lane and container price and check the sell side (routes/margin-rules.js).
  • Carrier offers come through an adapter with the spot-rates capability. Only the demo carrier's spot desk implements it (lib/dcsa-simulator/spot-desk.js), so live offers need Hapag-Lloyd Quick Quotes or Maersk Offers access.
  • Tests: spot-offers (52), spot-offers-carrier (41), Cypress spot-offers (8).
Carrier allocations & steering Full

New, 21 Oct. Space on routing lines was released in v0.91.10; the rest is unreleased.

  • Space configurations (routes/allocations.js) hold a contract's committed TEU on the routing lines they tick, optionally for one loop, customer or commodity. They hold it per period or per ISO week, nothing carried over (allocations.space_basis). Consumption is split into confirmed, pending and rejected, and a minimum commitment (MQC) is flagged.
  • Lane Steering orders carriers and contracts per trade lane, for everyone or one customer (routes/lane-steering.js).
  • Where to book walks that order with each option's free space in the ETD's week, pre-selects the first with space and explains every skipped option (lib/where-to-book.js).
  • The verdict: booking against the steering asks for a reason, and the server records the verdict on the shipment.
  • Reporting: Dashboard → Steering gives the steering rate by lane and carrier (GET /api/reports/steering).
  • Not built: MQC pace against elapsed time, and steering lines by region, country or port.
  • Tests: weekly-space (30), where-to-book (23), steering-verdict (21), Cypress where-to-book (4).
Rate benchmarking Partial

Corrected 21 Oct. src/pages/RateBenchmarkPage.jsx (since v0.70.0, August) compares every active contract's own rates for a lane side by side, cheapest total first, using the contract match. That is the same internal rate comparison credited to CargoWise as Partial in September; the September CargoDesk rating of None missed it. There is no market index and, as a single-tenant platform, no pooled data to build one from.
