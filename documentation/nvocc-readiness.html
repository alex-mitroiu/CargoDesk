CargoDesk · Systems Audit

NVOCC Readiness

An NVOCC is legally both a carrier and a shipper for the same physical move — carrier to its own customer on the House B/L, shipper to the real vessel operator on the Master B/L. This audits CargoDesk's data model against that dual role and closes what could be closed safely in one pass.

Audited 2026-08-19 · Epic TKT-Q52B38 · Findings 7 · Closed this pass 4
Where the ambiguity actually lives
Every field-level mapping break this audit found traces back to this one picture: CargoDesk's shipment record has one carrier slot and one principal slot, not one per document.

Findings

Ordered as audited. Closed entries shipped in this pass; open entries are logged as Epic TKT-Q52B38's backlog.

01 party-model
Closed

No way to record who the NVOCC even is

Nothing in the data model could name the NVOCC handling a shipment, let alone its license number — "Forwarder" is a related but different, broader relationship.

NVOCC needs

A first-class party role, resolvable to a real organization record.

Shipped

New "NVOCC" role in the existing extensible party system — assign any customer, same as Forwarder or Agent.

TKT-Q0I482
02 licensing
Closed

Zero FMC / tariff-license concept anywhere

An NVOCC operating in/out of the US must hold an FMC license and file a tariff; CargoDesk had no field to record that anywhere in the system.

NVOCC needs

A place to record the license/registration number backing its NVOCC status.

Shipped

customers.is_nvocc + fmc_number — a checkbox-reveals-field on the customer Profile tab.

TKT-Q0I482
03 booking-payload
Closed

The carrier booking named the wrong shipper

The outbound booking-request payload to the vessel operator always sent the underlying cargo owner as shipperName — legally, the NVOCC is the shipper of record on that side, not the cargo owner.

NVOCC needs

The Master B/L-side booking to name the NVOCC as shipper when one is handling the move.

Shipped

Booking payload now prefers the assigned NVOCC party's name; falls back to today's behavior otherwise.

TKT-ARVN18 · verified live — payload correctly read "Pacific Bridge NVOCC Ltd," not the real cargo owner
04 document-identity
Closed

The Bill of Lading never named who issued it

A Master B/L Number field already existed as a caption, but nothing on the document said which licensed party was actually standing behind it.

NVOCC needs

Its own identity visible on the House B/L it issues.

Shipped

An NVOCC detail row now renders on the generated Bill of Lading when one is assigned.

TKT-NZB9EI · verified live — captured the actual generated document HTML, confirmed the row renders
05 dual-document
Closed

Still one document, not two

A House B/L and a Master B/L are independently issued, independently signed documents with different parties in the Shipper/Consignee/Carrier slots. Today's single BL01 plus a caption field couldn't represent that.

NVOCC needs

Two real, separately generatable documents for one shipment.

Shipped

New MB01 Master Bill of Lading, its own builder — NVOCC as Shipper, Consignee reads "TO ORDER OF {NVOCC}", cross-referenced against the House B/L number.

TKT-ABO0TA · pulled forward from backlog on direct follow-up · verified live — generated MB01 HTML confirmed to carry the right title, parties, and cross-reference
06 structural-fields
Closed

Carrier and Principal are still single-slot

The exact ambiguity the sourcing brief warned about: carrier_code and principal_id each hold one value, with no structural way to separate "who we bought vessel capacity from" from "who we are, as carrier, to our own customer." A crosswalk against CargoWise/SALog/ZenCargo-style Principal fields breaks here.

NVOCC needs

Independent MBL-side and HBL-side carrier/principal resolution.

Today

One carrier_code, one principal_id — whichever side wrote last, wins.

TKT-9O2B3T · Status update — verified 2026-08-25: resolved narrower than originally scoped here, rather than the structural field split above. carrier_code keeps meaning the real vessel operator everywhere internally; the NVOCC identity that was missing is now surfaced on the one place it actually mattered — the public tracking page (routes/share.js adds nvoccName; TrackingPage.jsx renders the NVOCC as primary carrier, the real operator demoted to "via {carrierCode}"). See tests/nvocc-carrier-identity.test.js for the documented scoping conclusion.
07 destination-release
Closed

Cargo release is modeled as one event, not two

At destination, the vessel operator releases the container to the NVOCC's agent against the Master B/L; the NVOCC separately releases cargo to the real consignee against the House B/L. CargoDesk's release-type field and Delivery Order model only represent the second half.

NVOCC needs

Two distinct, sequenced release events against two different documents.

Today

One bl_release_type field, one Delivery Order.

TKT-IB5IEX · Status update — verified 2026-08-25: shipped as a real second release-event field, shipments.master_bl_release_type, exposed on the shipment form/Conditions page and rendered as a two-stage release note on the MB01 document.

Deliberately not re-litigated here

Two things this audit touches but doesn't reopen.

LCL / multi-shipper consolidation — already the largest known gap from the earlier competitive analysis, and deliberately deferred under the standing FCL-first roadmap decision. It overlaps with NVOCC support but isn't the same thing: the House/Master B/L split is real "even a single-shipper FCL container with zero consolidation involved," so Findings 01–04 above were correctly buildable without touching that decision.

NVOCC co-loading / cross-tariff reference — one NVOCC occasionally tenders cargo through another NVOCC's own tariff rather than direct to a vessel operator. Real, but less common than the direct case the rest of this audit targets — logged for completeness (TKT-UR1X17), not scoped in detail.
