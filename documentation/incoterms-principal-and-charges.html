Incoterms, the principal, and where a shipment's money goes

This page explains how the incoterm decides who the principal is, with the standard Incoterms 2020 table of who pays each transport step, and what the principal is used for. Then it covers how costs and charges are recorded, and which office and legal entity they belong to. It describes CargoDesk as it works today, including what it doesn't do yet.

In short

  • Each incoterm shares out the transport steps between the seller (the shipper) and the buyer (the consignee): the table in section 1. CargoDesk records the incoterm but doesn't share out charges by it.
  • The incoterm suggests the principal. With an E or F term (EXW, FCA, FAS, FOB) the buyer arranges the main carriage, so the consignee becomes the principal. With a C or D term (CPT, CIP, CFR, CIF, DAP, DPU, DDP) the seller pays it, so the shipper does. You can always choose someone else.
  • The principal is the customer the shipment is billed to. It sets the invoice currency, who is chased for payment and whose credit is checked. It also picks customer-specific contract space and charge defaults.
  • Each cost or charge line is marked Prepaid or Collect, by hand. The incoterm doesn't set this mark. It decides what the House B/L shows and what must be paid before the cargo is released at destination.
  • A shipment's costs and charges all belong to one legal entity: the export office's, or the import office's if the export office has none. CargoDesk doesn't divide a shipment's money between the export and the import office.

1. The incoterm and the principal it suggests

The incoterm is one of the eleven Incoterms 2020 rules. It is required on a new shipment and appears on the shipment header, on Conditions and on every invoice. CargoDesk uses it for one thing automatically: it suggests the principal. The rule asks who pays for the main carriage:

  1. FCA: at its own premises the seller loads the buyer’s vehicle and the buyer carries the goods on; at a terminal or another named place the seller carries them there and the buyer unloads them.
  2. FAS: the seller pays until the goods are alongside the vessel; loading them on board is the buyer’s.
  3. Destination terminal charges follow the seller’s contract of carriage. Under CFR and CIF it normally ends at the port of discharge, so the charges there are the buyer’s unless the freight includes them. Under CPT and CIP the seller’s carriage runs to the named place, so they are the seller’s when that place lies beyond the port.
  4. CPT and CIP: the seller pays the carriage to the named place of destination, inland if that is where it is named.
  5. Insurance: only CIF (minimum cover, Institute Cargo Clauses C) and CIP (all risks, clauses A) oblige the seller to insure. Under the other terms neither side has to; each insures the risk it carries.
  6. DAP and DDP deliver ready for unloading: when the named place is the destination terminal, unloading there is the buyer’s (DPU is the term that includes it).

CargoDesk records the incoterm on the shipment but doesn’t share out costs and charges by this table: each line is entered on the shipment as it comes (section 4). It uses the incoterm to suggest the principal, as in the first table.

2. How the principal is filled in

On New Shipment (and Edit Shipment):

  1. In Parties, choose the Shipper and the Consignee.
  2. Choose the Incoterm. CargoDesk puts the party from the table above into Principal.
  3. Check the principal. If someone else pays you, choose them instead: a parent company, a forwarder who buys the freight from you, or another third party. Any customer can be the principal.
  4. Save. Shipper, Consignee, Principal and Incoterm are all required on this form.

When the principal is not filled in for you

  • The party wasn't chosen yet. If you pick the incoterm before the shipper or consignee, the principal stays as it was. Choose it yourself, or pick the incoterm again.
  • You change the shipper or consignee afterwards. The principal doesn't follow; change it yourself if needed.
  • You change the incoterm on Conditions. That page changes the incoterm only; the principal stays.
  • The shipment was converted from a quote. It takes the quote's principal, which is optional on a quote and isn't set from the incoterm. Check it on Parties & Offices after converting.
  • Shipments created through the API keep whatever principal they were sent with. CargoDesk doesn't check it against the incoterm.

3. What the principal decides

The principal is the customer the shipment is sold to. Where no principal is set, CargoDesk falls back to the consignee for billing.

AreaWhat the principal decides
Invoice currencyInvoices are issued in the currency on the principal's customer profile, or US dollars if none is set (Invoice Entry tells you when it falls back).
Who owes the invoiceThe invoice's receivable is the principal's, so reminders go to the principal. They are sent on the principal's payment terms and reminder settings, and the collections alerts are about the principal's debt.
Credit controlA new invoice is refused while the principal, the shipper, the consignee or the contract's named account is on credit hold. The over-limit check is on the principal (the consignee when there is none); only the lane's trade manager can approve going over.
Tax details on the invoiceThe customer VAT number printed is the principal's (its primary VAT identifier, else any VAT, else a Tax ID).
Contract spaceWhen you look for a contract, space configurations made for this principal (its named account) are offered first. Lane Steering lines for this customer apply too, and Where to book follows them.
Charge DefaultsA Charge Default set up for this principal adds its charge and cost lines the first time Cost Entry opens. It is chosen before a general setup for the same lane.
StatementsA consolidated statement for a customer takes the unbilled charges of the shipments where that customer is the principal or the consignee.

Check the "Bill To" box on the invoice

The invoice's party box is labelled "Consignee / Bill To" and always shows the consignee. With a C or D term the principal is the shipper. The invoice is then still owed by the shipper, in the shipper's currency and with the shipper's VAT number, but the box names the consignee. Check the invoice before you send it in that case.

4. Costs and charges

A shipment's money is a list of lines on Accounting → Cost Entry and Invoice Entry. A BUY line is a cost: what you pay the carrier, a haulier or an agent. A SELL line is a charge: what you bill your customer. The gross profit is what you sell minus what you buy.

Where the lines come from

  • The contract: a Central contract's rates come in once, automatically, when Cost Entry first opens. A changed contract is applied through the Reconcile window.
  • Charge Defaults: set per principal, movement type and lane (Master Data → Finance).
  • A SPOT offer attached to the shipment.
  • Merchant's Haulage: the haulier's cost, entered on the haulage details.
  • The carrier's invoice, once approved in Freight Audit.
  • The carrier's Arrival Notice on the import side: "Add as accruals" adds its charges as BUY lines.
  • By hand, by either office: Cost Entry isn't limited to the export or the import side.

Prepaid or Collect, on each line

Every line is marked Prepaid (paid at origin) or Collect (paid at destination). It starts as Prepaid, and you change it on the line. Lines from an Arrival Notice take the carrier's own marking. The incoterm doesn't set it. Neither does the shipment's own Freight Terms (Prepaid or Collect, on Conditions), which is sent to the carrier with the booking and printed on the House B/L.

What the marking changes:

  • The House B/L printed with its charges shows each charge as prepaid or collect.
  • Releasing the cargo at destination needs every Collect charge on a paid invoice or statement first (an admin or finance user can override with a reason).
  • GP Overview splits the figures into prepaid and collect.

It does not send a line to the import office, or to a separate invoice. An invoice (FR01) takes every SELL line not yet billed, prepaid and collect together. You can also make one invoice per container.

5. Which office and legal entity the money belongs to

Every shipment has an export managing office and an import managing office, and sometimes a controlling office. Each office belongs to a legal entity (Organisation → Branches). This is how the money is assigned:

WhatBelongs to
All of the shipment's costs and charges (margin, VAT, GL export, FX revaluation)The export office's legal entity; if that office has none, the import office's. One entity for the whole shipment.
Sending invoices and documents by emailThe export office's mail account.
Payment reminders, collections alerts and escalationThe export office's mail account, thresholds and branch manager. A shipment without an export office gets no automatic reminders.
A consolidated statementOne legal entity per statement. It is chased by the export office holding the largest share of its value.
Entering cost and charge linesEither office: anyone who can edit the shipment.

What CargoDesk doesn't do today

It doesn't divide one shipment's costs and charges between the export and the import office. Destination charges entered by the import office still count for the export office's legal entity. There is also no billing between the two offices (agent invoices or a profit share), and no separate profit and loss for each side. Prepaid and Collect don't change this.
