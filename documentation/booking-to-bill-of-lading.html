Booking to Bill of Lading

If a business ran FCL-only ocean freight forwarding and had to pick one platform to actually move shipments on — CargoWise One or CargoDesk — where does CargoDesk fall short on the shipment process itself: booking, tracking, documenting, and clearing a container from origin to destination? Not billing, not BI, not the customer portal. Just handling the shipment.

01 Booking & Carrier Connectivity

Sending a booking to the carrier and getting real rates back — the first step of every shipment.

Live carrier booking, no real connection behind it
Critical
CargoWise
Direct carrier REST APIs (Hapag-Lloyd, CMA CGM, ONE) plus eAdaptor XML/SOAP EDI — IFTMIN booking submission, IFTMBF confirmation — with INTTRA / Infor Nexus / CargoSmart as a neutral-hub fallback for carriers without a direct link.
CargoDesk
POST /edi-messages/booking-request builds a genuinely complete payload (equipment summary, DG declaration, contract ref, rate snapshot) — but the only thing that ever answers it is Test Tools' own manual simulator. No carrier, anywhere, is actually connected.
Why it matters: a forwarder sending dozens of bookings a week across several carriers needs a real send-and-confirm loop, not a developer console standing in for one.
VGM is a status flip, not a SOLAS filing
Major
CargoWise
VERMAS EDI message actually transmits Verified Gross Mass to the carrier/terminal ahead of the cutoff.
CargoDesk
containers.vgm_status is a real field with real cutoff-badge logic, but "Submitted" only ever changes a row in CargoDesk's own database — nothing leaves the building.
Why it matters: VGM is a legal SOLAS obligation to the carrier, not internal bookkeeping — a green badge in CargoDesk proves nothing to the port.
Contract rates never arrive on their own
Minor
CargoWise
Carriers (e.g. ONE) push contract rates and published tariff surcharges directly into the platform.
CargoDesk
contract_rates is a solid, well-modeled table (multi-routing, per-lane, IMDG-aware) — entirely hand-entered via the MDM form. Status update, verified 2026-08-25: no CSV-import path exists anywhere in the codebase for contract rates — the original claim that it was "hand-entered or CSV-imported" overstated this; only OFAC/CSL sanctions data has a CSV-import route. A carrier rate change never reaches it on its own.
Why it matters: manageable on a small book; a real drag once contract volume and update frequency grow.
02 Schedule & Vessel Visibility

Finding a real, bookable sailing — and knowing where the vessel actually is afterward.

Sailing search has no real carrier behind it
Critical
CargoWise
Real-time schedule search across leading carriers, with live ETD/ETA flowing straight into the job.
CargoDesk
GET /schedules/search is catalog-first (real, previously-saved sailings), falling back to synthetic "DEMO …" results. A live Maersk-API tier existed once and was deliberately removed in v0.72.1 — no environment ever had a working key.
Why it matters: booking a real shipment starts with picking a real sailing. CargoDesk cannot show a customer one today.
AIS vessel tracking — the one place CargoDesk is genuinely live
Minor / partial parity
CargoWise
Global AIS coverage feeding automated ETD/ETA updates and delay notifications directly into the workflow.
CargoDesk
A real, persistent aisstream.io WebSocket (lib/ais-listener.js) resolves vessel identity and confirms a tracked leg's actual ETD/ETA in place, with provenance (etd_source='ais') — genuinely live data, just narrower in scope than a full schedule feed.
Why it matters: worth stating plainly — this is the one shipment-process area where CargoDesk isn't simulating anything.
No electronic port/terminal connection
Major
CargoWise
Electronic Port Connection — COPARN/BAPLIE terminal-layer EDI updates container movement automatically from the terminal itself.
CargoDesk
container_events (Empty Pickup → Gate In → Loaded → Sailed → Discharged → Gate Out → Empty Return) is a real, well-modeled lifecycle log feeding demurrage/detention math — every event is typed in by an operator by hand.
Why it matters: free-time and cutoff exposure is only as current as whoever remembered to log in and type an event.
03 Milestone Tracking & Exceptions

Knowing a shipment has slipped — before the customer has to ask.

Milestones are hand-completed, not EDI-driven
Major
CargoWise
IFTSTA messages update milestones automatically; 2026 doubled down on linking container milestones directly to automated workflow triggers and delay notifications.
CargoDesk
shipment_milestones is a real planned-vs-actual model (9 fixed steps, estimated vs completed date) — structurally close to an actual Cargo iQ Route Map — but is either completed by hand or by a handful of internal auto-triggers. No external carrier or terminal feed ever completes one.
Why it matters: this is exactly the gap the Command Center's new exception-management epic (TKT-IBHB0K) targets — but that epic aggregates what's already there; it doesn't make the milestones themselves arrive automatically.
No proactive delay notification to the customer
Minor
CargoWise
Automatic delay notifications ride on the same real-time tracking feed.
CargoDesk
The public tracking link (routes/share.js) shows current data whenever a customer checks it — nothing pushes a notice out when a milestone slips.
Why it matters: "the system knew three days ago" is a bad conversation to have with a customer.
04 Trade Documentation

The Bill of Lading isn't paperwork — it's the title document. This is where "signed" and "electronic" stop meaning the same thing.

A signed PDF is not an eBL
Critical
CargoWise
Real electronic Bills of Lading aligned with DCSA, FIATA, ICC, BIMCO, SWIFT and FIT Alliance standards, approved by the International Group of P&I Clubs — legally negotiable, and able to effect cargo release and trade finance on their own.
CargoDesk
A genuinely tamper-evident, CAdES/PKCS#7-signed PDF Bill of Lading — real cryptographic proof CargoDesk authored it unaltered. That is not the same claim as a DCSA-standard eBL: it has no legal negotiability, and no carrier, bank, or P&I club recognizes it as a title document.
Why it matters: for anything on Telex Release, Surrendered, or negotiable terms at real volume, this is the single largest "looks similar, isn't" gap in the whole stack — and it needs an accredited eBL platform relationship, not more code inside CargoDesk.
Document capture is one call site, not an inbox
Minor
CargoWise
Automated document capture across the inbound document flow, streamlining FCL data entry generally.
CargoDesk
A real vision-model extraction endpoint (POST /ai/extract-document, v0.70.0) exists and genuinely works — but it's wired to exactly one place: the Freight Audit carrier-invoice intake.
Why it matters: the hard part (a working extraction call) is already solved — this is a wiring gap, not a capability gap.
05 Customs & Regulatory Filing

Getting the container legally across a border — anywhere but the US.

One country's filings, entirely simulated
Critical
CargoWise
Cited directly as a comparative strength — deeper configurability and broader non-US customs support, with real electronic government filing integrations, built for global enterprises.
CargoDesk
customs_filings is explicitly, deliberately scoped as simulated/mock-only — US AES/EEI export and ISF/AMS import, full stop. Zero real government EDI connection. Zero non-US regime modeled at all.
Why it matters: a hard blocker for real compliance on any lane that isn't US-only — which, for an FCL forwarder, is most lanes.
Verdict — if I had to choose today

CargoWise wins the operational spine. CargoDesk wins everything built on top of it.

Four of the gaps above aren't things CargoDesk's own engineering can close by itself: a live carrier connection, a real-time schedule feed, a DCSA-accredited eBL, and multi-country government customs EDI all require a commercial relationship — a carrier API agreement, an INTTRA/e2open subscription, an eBL platform partnership, a customs-broker credential per country — not another sprint. That's the honest reason the earlier competitive-gap-analysis work in this project (v0.69.0) named several gaps as structurally out of reach rather than backlog items, and the same logic applies here.

For a business actually transacting real bookings with real carriers across real borders today, CargoWise is the only one of the two that can actually run the shipment end to end without a human standing in for the carrier, the terminal, and the customs authority.

What CargoDesk already does well sits one layer up from all of this: once a shipment's data exists — parties, cargo, containers, milestones, cost lines — the depth of the FCL data model (per-pack-item HS codes and value, container lifecycle events tied to real demurrage/detention math, a 13-role party model, multi-list compliance screening, NVOCC dual-identity documents, credit control) is genuinely strong, and every part of it is fully own-able and extensible because CargoDesk is the team's own code. That's real value — it just isn't the same question as "can this system talk to a real carrier today," which is what handling an actual shipment ultimately requires.
